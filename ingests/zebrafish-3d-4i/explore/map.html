Where the zip keeps its table of contents

A zip's table of contents, the central directory, is at the end of the file, after the last member. It holds one entry per member, in the order the members are stored: the name, the sizes, the compression method, a CRC and the offset of the member's local header. Three short records after it say where it starts, so a reader works backwards from the last byte:

  1. The last 22 bytes are the end of central directory record (signature PK\x05\x06). Here its entry count and offset are 0xffff and 0xffffffff, which mean "too large for 16 or 32 bits: see the ZIP64 record".
  2. The 20 bytes before it are the ZIP64 locator (PK\x06\x07), which gives the offset of the ZIP64 end record.
  3. The ZIP64 end record (PK\x06\x06, 56 bytes) gives the true entry count and the directory's offset and size.
  4. Then the central directory itself. The tools keep it as a Parquet file, one row per entry.

The bar is the central directory, split by the well each entry describes. An entry is 46 bytes, plus the member's name, plus its extra field: 9 bytes (a timestamp), or 21 for a member past 4 GiB, whose 64-bit offset is stored there.

Reading a member's name

Every member's name is a Zarr v2 key. After the array's path come the chunk's indices, one per axis, in the array's axis order: (c, z, y, x) for an image, (z, y, x) for a label. A chunk is 1 channel × 10 planes × 1000 × 1000 pixels, so the z index counts groups of 10 planes and the y and x indices count 1000 pixels.

An image chunk: one channel of one round

A label chunk: one segmentation, with no channel axis

Names ending in .zarray (an array's shape, chunk shape, data type and compressor), .zattrs (attributes: pyramid scales, channel names, plate and well lists) or .zgroup (marks a group) are metadata. Names containing /tables/ belong to AnnData tables such as FOV_ROI_table.
